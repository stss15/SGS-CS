## Topic summary
This topic helps you meet your MacBook safely and confidently. You will learn the main parts, how to use the trackpad and keyboard, and how to log in and manage power.

## Learning goals
- Identify the main ports and buttons on your MacBook.
- Use the trackpad to click, right click, and scroll.
- Use the keyboard home row and essential keys.
- Log in safely and choose sleep or shut down.

## Lesson 1.1.1: Physical Setup and Ports
### Keywords
- MacBook: The laptop you use at school.
- Port (USB-C): A small oval socket for charging and connecting devices.
- MagSafe: A magnetic charger that clicks into place (if your model has it).
- Power button: The key used to turn the MacBook on.
- Headphone jack: The round socket for headphones (if your model has it).

### Core content
Your MacBook has a few important ports and buttons. Find the USB-C ports on the left or right side. These ports are used for charging and for plugging in other devices. If your MacBook uses MagSafe, the charging cable clicks on with a magnet and shows a light.
The power button is on the keyboard (top right). Press it once to turn the MacBook on. If you have Touch ID, this button also reads your fingerprint.
Carry the MacBook with two hands and close the lid before you move. Never lift it by the screen.

### Key reminders
- Check the charging light before you unplug.
- Keep the lid closed when you carry your MacBook.
- Never lift by the screen.

### Try it
- Point to each port on your MacBook and say its name.
- Plug in the charger and check the light color.
- Practice carrying the MacBook with two hands.

### Quick check
1. Which port do you use to charge your MacBook?
2. What does an orange charging light mean?
3. Why should you not lift a MacBook by the screen?

### Did you know
MagSafe is magnetic so it disconnects safely if someone trips on the cable.

### Common pitfalls
- Unplugging before the light shows charging.
- Lifting the MacBook by the screen.

### Exam tip
If asked which port charges your MacBook, the answer is USB-C.

## Lesson 1.1.2: Trackpad Gestures and Clicks
### Keywords
- Trackpad: The flat touch surface used to control the cursor.
- Click: Pressing down to select something.
- Two-finger tap: A light tap with two fingers for right click.
- Scroll: Moving two fingers to move the page.
- Force Touch: A firm press that opens extra options (optional).

### Core content
Use one finger to move the cursor. Tap or press once to click. To open a file, double click quickly.
A two-finger tap is the same as a right click. It opens a menu with extra options. Use two fingers to scroll up and down a page.
Force Touch is a firmer press that opens extra options. You can ignore this until you feel confident.

### Key reminders
- Two-finger tap is your right click.
- Use light taps, not heavy presses.
- Scrolling uses two fingers.

### Try it
- Open Finder and click once on a folder.
- Two-finger tap the folder to open the menu.
- Scroll up and down in any page with two fingers.

### Quick check
1. What does a two-finger tap do?
2. How do you scroll on a trackpad?
3. When do you use a double click?

### Did you know
The trackpad can detect up to five fingers at once.

### Common pitfalls
- Confusing single click with double click.
- Pressing too hard when a light tap is enough.

### Exam tip
The gesture that acts like an iPad long press is a two-finger tap.

## Lesson 1.1.3: Keyboard Layout and Essential Keys
### Keywords
- Keyboard: The keys you use to type.
- Home row: The middle row of keys where your fingers rest (ASDF JKL;).
- Command key: The main shortcut key on a Mac (Cmd).
- Option key: A modifier key used with other keys.
- Control key: A modifier key used with other keys.
- Caps Lock: Turns capital letters on or off.
- Return: The key used to start a new line or confirm.
- Delete: The key that removes the letter before the cursor.

### Core content
Place your fingers on the home row: left hand on ASDF and right hand on JKL;. The F and J keys have small bumps to help you find the home row without looking.
The Command key (Cmd) is the main shortcut key. Option and Control are also used with shortcuts.
Caps Lock has a small light. It should be off when you type passwords. The Return key starts a new line or confirms a choice. The Delete key removes the letter to the left of the cursor. To delete the letter to the right, hold Fn and press Delete.

### Key reminders
- Use the bumps on F and J to find the home row.
- Check Caps Lock before typing a password.

### Try it
- Place your fingers on ASDF and JKL; and type your name.
- Find the Command, Option, and Control keys.
- Turn Caps Lock on and off and watch the light.

### Quick check
1. Which keys are the home row?
2. What is the main shortcut key on a Mac?
3. What does the Delete key remove?

### Did you know
The QWERTY layout was designed in the 1870s to prevent typewriter keys from jamming.

### Common pitfalls
- Looking at the keyboard instead of the screen.
- Leaving Caps Lock on when typing passwords.

### Exam tip
If asked for the main shortcut key, the answer is Command (Cmd).

## Lesson 1.1.4: Logging In and Power States
### Keywords
- Login: Signing in with your school account.
- Password: A secret word you type to access your account.
- Touch ID: Fingerprint sign-in (if enabled).
- Sleep mode: A low power state that keeps work open.
- Shut down: Turning the MacBook fully off.

### Core content
To log in, type your school username and password. The password shows as dots to keep it private. Never share your password.
If Touch ID is set up, you can use your fingerprint to log in. Always save your work before you sleep or shut down.
Sleep mode keeps your work open and uses a little battery. Shut down closes all apps and uses no battery.

### Key reminders
- Look at the screen while you type your password.
- Save before you sleep or shut down.

### Try it
- Find the password box on the login screen and type a few letters to see the dots.
- Open the Apple menu and find Sleep and Shut Down.

### Quick check
1. Why does the password show as dots?
2. Which power state keeps your work open?
3. What should you do before shutting down?

### Did you know
Touch ID stores your fingerprint securely on the MacBook and does not share it.

### Common pitfalls
- Sharing passwords with friends.
- Shutting down without saving.

### Exam tip
Sleep keeps your open work ready to continue.

## Lesson 1.1.5: Sleep vs Shut Down
### Keywords
- Sleep: Low power mode that keeps work open.
- Shut down: Full power off.
- Battery: The power stored in your MacBook.
- Power: Energy used to run the device.

### Core content
Use Sleep for short breaks. The MacBook wakes quickly and your work stays open.
Use Shut Down when you will not use the MacBook for a long time. It uses no battery and starts fresh next time.
Sleep still uses a little battery. If you sleep overnight, keep the charger connected.

### Key reminders
- Sleep is quick, shut down is safe for long storage.
- Plug in if the MacBook will sleep for many hours.

### Try it
- Put the MacBook to sleep and wake it again.
- With teacher permission, shut down and start up once.

### Quick check
1. Which mode uses no battery?
2. Which mode is best for a short break?
3. Why should you plug in if sleeping overnight?

### Did you know
Modern MacBooks can stay in sleep for weeks on a full charge.

### Common pitfalls
- Leaving the MacBook asleep for days without charging.

### Exam tip
Sleep uses battery; shut down does not.

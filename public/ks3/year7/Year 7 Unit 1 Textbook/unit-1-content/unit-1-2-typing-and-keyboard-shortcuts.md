## Topic summary
This topic builds confident typing habits and introduces the most useful keyboard shortcuts. You will learn correct posture, practice accuracy, and use shortcuts that save time in every subject.

## Learning goals
- Sit and type with safe, comfortable posture.
- Use the home row and build muscle memory.
- Use core shortcuts for copy, paste, save, select all, and undo.
- Take and manage screenshots.
- Understand the clipboard and undo.

## Lesson 1.2.1: Touch Typing Posture and Home Row
### Keywords
- Touch typing: Typing without looking at the keys.
- Home row: The middle row of keys (ASDF JKL;).
- Posture: How you sit and hold your body while working.

### Core content
Good posture helps you type comfortably and avoid pain. Sit with your feet flat on the floor and your back straight. Keep your elbows about 90 degrees and your wrists level, not bent up or down. The top of the screen should be around eye level.
Place your fingers on the home row. Use the bumps on F and J to find the correct position. Your thumbs rest on the space bar. This position helps your fingers learn where keys are without looking.

### Key reminders
- Do not rest your wrists on the desk edge.
- Short practice every day is better than one long session.

### Try it
- Sit correctly and place your fingers on ASDF JKL;.
- Type the home row letters slowly and correctly.

### Quick check
1. What are the home row keys?
2. Why do F and J have bumps?
3. What should your wrists look like while typing?

### Did you know
Professional typists can average 75 words per minute with high accuracy.

### Common pitfalls
- Looking at the keys instead of the screen.
- Slouching or bending wrists.

### Exam tip
The home row keys are ASDF JKL;.

## Lesson 1.2.2: Building Typing Speed and Accuracy
### Keywords
- Accuracy: Typing the correct letters.
- Speed: How fast you type.
- Muscle memory: Your fingers learning positions through practice.

### Core content
Accuracy comes first. When you type the right letters, your speed will improve naturally. Practice short phrases, your name, and simple sentences. Use a typing test tool to track words per minute (WPM) and accuracy.
Correct mistakes as soon as you see them. This builds good habits and stops errors from becoming permanent.

### Key reminders
- Slow and accurate is better than fast and messy.
- Practice for 10 minutes a day to improve.

### Try it
- Take a 1 minute typing test and write down your accuracy.
- Practice the same sentence twice and try to reduce mistakes.

### Quick check
1. Why should you focus on accuracy first?
2. What does WPM mean?
3. How long should a daily practice session be?

### Did you know
The world record for typing speed is over 200 words per minute.

### Common pitfalls
- Rushing and making lots of errors.
- Ignoring mistakes instead of fixing them.

### Exam tip
Accuracy builds correct muscle memory.

## Lesson 1.2.3: Core Keyboard Shortcuts
### Keywords
- Keyboard shortcut: A key combination that does a task quickly.
- Copy: Duplicate selected text or a file.
- Paste: Place copied text or a file.
- Save: Store your work.
- Command key: The main shortcut key on a Mac (Cmd).

### Core content
Shortcuts save time in every subject. Hold the Command key and tap the letter key:
- Cmd+C = Copy
- Cmd+V = Paste
- Cmd+S = Save
- Cmd+A = Select all
- Cmd+Z = Undo
To use a shortcut, keep Command pressed, tap the other key, then release both.

### Key reminders
- Memorize these five shortcuts.
- Practice in any text box.

### Try it
- Type a sentence, highlight it, and use Cmd+C then Cmd+V.
- Save your work with Cmd+S.

### Quick check
1. What does Cmd+S do?
2. Which shortcut selects all text?
3. How do you undo a mistake?

### Did you know
The Command symbol comes from Scandinavian road signs.

### Common pitfalls
- Letting go of Command too early.
- Using the mouse for everything instead of shortcuts.

### Exam tip
Cmd+S means Save.

## Lesson 1.2.4: Screenshot Techniques
### Keywords
- Screenshot: A picture of your screen.
- Cmd+Shift+3: Full screen screenshot.
- Cmd+Shift+4: Select part of the screen.
- Desktop: The main screen area where files can appear.

### Core content
Use Cmd+Shift+3 to capture the whole screen. Use Cmd+Shift+4 to capture a selected area. Your cursor becomes a crosshair; drag to select the area. Screenshots save to the Desktop by default.
Rename screenshots and move them into a folder so your Desktop stays clean. Press Esc to cancel a screenshot.

### Key reminders
- The crosshair means you are selecting an area.
- Delete old screenshots you do not need.

### Try it
- Take a full screen screenshot.
- Take a small area screenshot and rename the file.

### Quick check
1. Which shortcut captures the whole screen?
2. Where do screenshots save by default?
3. How do you cancel a screenshot?

### Did you know
Screenshots are saved as PNG files by default.

### Common pitfalls
- Forgetting where screenshots are saved.
- Leaving many screenshots on the Desktop.

### Exam tip
Cmd+Shift+3 captures the whole screen.

## Lesson 1.2.5: Clipboard and Undo Operations
### Keywords
- Clipboard: Temporary storage for one copied item.
- Undo: Reverse your last action.
- Redo: Undo the undo.
- Cmd+Z: Undo shortcut.
- Cmd+Shift+Z: Redo shortcut.

### Core content
When you copy something, it goes to the clipboard. The clipboard holds one item at a time. If you copy a new item, the old one is replaced.
Use Cmd+Z to undo mistakes in most apps. Use Cmd+Shift+Z to redo. Undo is a safe way to fix errors without deleting lots of work.

### Key reminders
- Paste before you copy something new.
- Undo works in almost every app.

### Try it
- Copy a word, paste it, then copy a new word and paste again.
- Make a mistake and use Cmd+Z to fix it.

### Quick check
1. How many items can the clipboard hold?
2. What does Cmd+Z do?
3. How do you redo an undo?

### Did you know
The idea of a clipboard was first used in early computers in the 1970s.

### Common pitfalls
- Thinking the clipboard saves many items.
- Forgetting to paste before copying again.

### Exam tip
Cmd+Z is Undo.

## Lesson 1.2.6: Stretch and Challenge - Custom Shortcuts and Text Replacement
### Keywords
- Custom shortcut: A shortcut you create yourself.
- Text replacement: An abbreviation that expands into full text.
- System Settings: The app where you change Mac settings.

### Core content
You can create your own shortcuts and text replacements in System Settings under Keyboard. For example, you can set "omw" to become "On my way". These settings work across most apps.
Only change settings if your teacher says it is okay. Custom shortcuts should not conflict with common shortcuts like Cmd+C or Cmd+S.

### Key reminders
- Do not change system settings without permission.
- Choose abbreviations you will remember.

### Try it
- Plan three text replacements you could use for school.
- Explain why each one saves time.

### Quick check
1. Where do you set text replacements?
2. Why should you avoid conflicting shortcuts?

### Did you know
You can create a short code for your school email address.

### Common pitfalls
- Creating a shortcut that breaks another command.

### Exam tip
Text replacements work in most apps once they are set.

## Topic summary
This topic shows you how to open apps, control windows, and switch between tasks. You will learn the traffic light buttons, how to quit apps, and how to use split view and multiple desktops.

## Learning goals
- Open apps using the Dock, Launchpad, and Spotlight.
- Use the window control buttons correctly.
- Quit apps when you are finished.
- Switch between apps and manage multiple windows.
- Use full screen, split view, and multiple desktops.

## Lesson 1.4.1: Opening Applications (Dock, Launchpad, Spotlight)
### Keywords
- Dock: The row of app icons at the bottom of the screen.
- Launchpad: A grid that shows all apps.
- Spotlight: A search tool that opens apps quickly.
- Application: A program you use on your MacBook.

### Core content
The Dock shows your most used apps. Click an icon to open the app. A small dot under the icon means the app is running.
Launchpad shows every app on your MacBook. Open it with the Launchpad key (F4 on some keyboards) or a pinch with thumb and three fingers on the trackpad.
Spotlight is the fastest way to open any app. Press Cmd+Space, type the app name, then press Return.

### Key reminders
- Use Spotlight for apps that are not in the Dock.
- The dot under an icon means the app is open.

### Try it
- Open Word using the Dock.
- Open OneNote using Spotlight.

### Quick check
1. Which tool shows all apps in a grid?
2. What shortcut opens Spotlight?
3. What does the dot under a Dock icon mean?

### Did you know
Spotlight can also do quick calculations and conversions.

### Common pitfalls
- Searching for apps in Finder instead of Spotlight.

### Exam tip
Cmd+Space opens Spotlight.

## Lesson 1.4.2: Window Control Buttons (Traffic Lights)
### Keywords
- Traffic lights: The red, yellow, and green buttons on a window.
- Close: Red button, closes the window.
- Minimize: Yellow button, hides the window in the Dock.
- Full screen: Green button, makes the window fill the screen.

### Core content
Every window has three buttons in the top left corner. Red closes the window. Yellow minimizes it to the Dock. Green makes the window full screen.
Closing a window does not always quit the app. If the app is still running, you will see a dot under its icon in the Dock.

### Key reminders
- Red closes a window, not the app.
- Full screen hides the menu bar; move the cursor to the top to see it.

### Try it
- Open a window and try each button.
- Minimize a window and restore it from the Dock.

### Quick check
1. Which button hides a window?
2. Which button makes full screen?
3. Does the red button quit the app?

### Did you know
The red, yellow, and green buttons are called traffic lights.

### Common pitfalls
- Thinking the red button quits the app.

### Exam tip
The yellow button minimizes the window.

## Lesson 1.4.3: Quitting vs Closing Applications
### Keywords
- Quit: Fully close an app.
- Close: Close a window only.
- Dot: A small mark under a Dock icon that shows an app is running.
- Cmd+Q: Shortcut to quit an app.

### Core content
Closing a window only hides it. The app can still run in the background. To fully close an app, use Cmd+Q or right click the Dock icon and choose Quit.
Quitting apps you are not using saves memory and helps your MacBook run smoothly.

### Key reminders
- Look for dots under icons to see running apps.
- Quit apps when you are finished.

### Try it
- Close a window and check for the dot.
- Quit the app with Cmd+Q.

### Quick check
1. How do you fully quit an app?
2. What does a dot under the Dock icon mean?
3. Why should you quit unused apps?

### Did you know
Quitting apps frees memory and improves performance.

### Common pitfalls

### Exam tip
Cmd+Q quits an app.

## Lesson 1.4.4: App Switching and Multitasking
### Keywords
- App switcher: The list of open apps.
- Cmd+Tab: Shortcut to switch apps.
- Mission Control: View all open windows.
- Active app: The app you are currently using.

### Core content
Use Cmd+Tab to switch between open apps. Hold Cmd and tap Tab to move through the list. Use Cmd+Shift+Tab to move backward.
Mission Control shows all open windows. Use a three-finger swipe up on the trackpad to open it. The menu bar always shows the active app.

### Key reminders
- Cmd+Tab is the fastest way to switch apps.
- Mission Control shows every window at once.

### Try it
- Switch between two apps using Cmd+Tab.
- Open Mission Control and choose a window.

### Quick check
1. How do you open the app switcher?
2. What does Mission Control show?
3. Where can you see the active app name?

### Did you know
Mission Control used to be called Expose.

### Common pitfalls

### Exam tip
Use Mission Control to see all open windows.

## Lesson 1.4.5: Split View and Full Screen
### Keywords
- Split View: Two apps side by side.
- Full screen: One app fills the screen.
- Multitasking: Using more than one app.

### Core content
Click the green button to go full screen, or press Cmd+Ctrl+F. For Split View, hold the green button and choose left or right, then select a second app.
To leave Split View, drag the divider all the way to one side or press the green button again. Full screen apps live in their own space, and you can swipe left or right with three fingers to switch.

### Key reminders
- Split View is helpful for research and writing.
- You can exit Split View by dragging the divider.

### Try it
- Put Word and a browser into Split View.
- Exit Split View and return to normal windows.

### Quick check
1. How do you enter Split View?
2. How do you leave Split View?
3. Which shortcut makes full screen?

### Did you know
You can have several full screen spaces and swipe between them.

### Common pitfalls
- Feeling stuck in Split View.

### Exam tip
Split View shows two apps side by side.

## Lesson 1.4.6: Stretch and Challenge - Mission Control and Multiple Desktops
### Keywords
- Desktop (Space): A separate workspace in Mission Control.
- Spaces: Another name for multiple desktops.
- Control+Left/Right: Shortcut to switch desktops.

### Core content
Open Mission Control with a three-finger swipe up. Click the plus sign to add a new desktop. Drag windows to different desktops to organize your work.
Use Control+Left or Control+Right to move between desktops. Keep a simple system, like one desktop for classwork and one for research.

### Key reminders
- Too many desktops can be confusing. Start with two or three.
- Name your desktops by purpose in your mind.

### Try it
- Create a second desktop and move a window to it.
- Switch between desktops with Control+Left or Control+Right.

### Quick check
1. How do you create a new desktop?
2. Which keys switch between desktops?
3. Why should you limit the number of desktops?

### Did you know
You can create many desktops, but two or three is best for beginners.

### Common pitfalls
- Forgetting which desktop has which app.

### Exam tip
Mission Control lets you manage multiple desktops.

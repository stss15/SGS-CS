## Unit Plan Extract (Context)
```text
Unit Plan: HL Unit 10 - The HL Case Study
Subject: IB Computer Science (First Assessment 2027)
Grade: Year 13 (HL Only)
Duration: 3 Weeks (8 HL Lessons)
Theme: Case Study (Paper 1, Section B)
Unit Foundation
Concepts The focus is on Synthesis and Strategic Justification. HL students must go beyond understanding the emerging technology to evaluating it within a global, multi-layered framework. Key themes include Operational Constraints (e.g., latency, battery life), Scalability, and the Interplay of Conflicting Factors (e.g., efficiency vs. privacy).
Content
	•	Deep Research: Investigating the four challenge questions (2 SL + 2 HL-specific).
	•	Technical Synthesis: Linking research papers and white papers to the core technical concepts of the syllabus (A1, A2, A3, A4, B3, B4).
	•	Strategy Formulation: Drafting and justifying comprehensive strategies for the case study organisation (e.g., "BotPro").
	•	Real-world Context: Mapping theoretical research to documented real-world deployments and expert findings (e.g., vSLAM in fire rescue).
Skills for Learning
	•	Research Skills: Academic literacy in navigating technical journals and industry reports to find "expert" evidence.
	•	Thinking Skills: Critical evaluation of solutions—balancing technical complexity against practical operational timeframes and resource availability.
Approaches to Teaching
	•	Inquiry-led: Seminar-style discussions where students present their findings on specific HL challenge questions.
	•	Informed by Assessment: Systematic practice of the 12-mark HL essay question, focusing on "balanced accounts" as per IB subject reports.
	•	Collaborative: Creating a shared "Evidence Bank" of research paper citations and real-world disaster/emergency scenarios.
Terminology
Pre-seen, HL Challenge Questions, Synthesis, Operational Timeframe, Computational Complexity, Sensor Data Integrity, Resource Management, Strategic Justification, Peer-reviewed Research.
Misconceptions
	•	Quantity over Quality: Believing that listing many technologies is better than deep evaluation. (IB Feedback: "better responses provided a balanced account").
	•	Ethics vs. Logic: Weaker responses often drift into purely ethical debate without addressing the underlying computational complexity or hardware constraints.
	•	Isolation: Forgetting that the Case Study is part of the CS curriculum; students must link findings to specific syllabus content (e.g., how vSLAM uses Matrix math or ADTs).
Adaptive Strategies
	•	Complex Information Mapping: Use "Decision Matrices" to help students evaluate two competing solutions (e.g., LiDAR vs. vSLAM) across five technical criteria.
	•	Essay Scaffolding: Provide a "Counter-Argument" checklist to ensure students move from "describing" to "evaluating" in their long-form writing.
	•	Executive Function Support: Use a "Research Log" template with discrete headings for Source, Technical Detail, and Evaluation.
TOK Connections
	•	Expertise and Authority: How do we weigh the "knowledge" provided by a commercial company (marketing white paper) against that of an academic researcher?
	•	Predictive Knowledge: To what extent can we rely on "simulated" research to predict the success of a technology in a "messy" real-world emergency?
IBO Learner Profile
	•	Knowledgeable: Demonstrating depth of understanding by referencing specific research papers and technical breakthroughs.
	•	Reflective: Critically assessing the limitations of proposed technologies in time-critical, high-stress environments.
CAS Connections
	•	Creativity: Producing a "Future Tech Briefing" for the school’s IT department or leadership team based on case study insights.
```

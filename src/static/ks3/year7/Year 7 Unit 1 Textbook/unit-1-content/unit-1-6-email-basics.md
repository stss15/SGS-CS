## Topic summary
This topic teaches you when to use email, how to write a clear message, and how to manage your inbox. You will also learn safe and respectful online behavior.

## Learning goals
- Choose email or Teams for the right situation.
- Identify the parts of an email.
- Write a clear, polite email.
- Attach files correctly.
- Keep your inbox organized and safe.

## Lesson 1.6.1: Email vs Teams - When to Use Each
### Keywords
- Email: Formal written communication.
- Teams: Quick messages and collaboration.
- Formal: Polite, respectful, and complete.
- Communication: Sharing information with others.

### Core content
Use email for formal messages, especially when writing to teachers. Email is good for longer messages, attachments, and official requests.
Use Teams for quick questions, class chats, and group work. If you need a record of a request or you are sending work, email is usually best.

### Key reminders
- Use email for teachers and formal requests.
- Use Teams for quick class messages.

### Try it
- Decide whether each scenario needs email or Teams.
- Explain your choice to a partner.

### Quick check
1. When should you use email instead of Teams?
2. Which tool is best for a quick class question?
3. Why is email useful for formal requests?

### Did you know
The first email was sent in 1971 as a test between two computers.

### Common pitfalls
- Using Teams for formal requests.

### Exam tip
Use email for formal communication with teachers.

## Lesson 1.6.2: Email Anatomy (To, CC, BCC, Subject)
### Keywords
- To: The main recipient.
- CC: A copy for other people who should see the email.
- BCC: Hidden recipients.
- Subject: A short summary of the email.
- Recipient: The person who receives the email.

### Core content
The To field is for the main person you are emailing. CC is for people who should be informed. BCC hides recipients from each other and is used for large groups.
The subject should be short and clear. A good subject helps your teacher respond quickly.

### Key reminders
- Use clear subjects like "Year 7 homework question".
- Do not use BCC unless a teacher asks you to.

### Try it
- Write a good subject line for an email to a teacher.
- Explain who belongs in To and who belongs in CC.

### Quick check
1. What is the purpose of the subject line?
2. Who goes in the To field?
3. What does BCC mean?

### Did you know
CC stands for Carbon Copy from the days of paper copies.

### Common pitfalls
- Writing a vague subject like "Help".

### Exam tip
The subject line should be specific and clear.

## Lesson 1.6.3: Writing a Clear Email Message
### Keywords
- Compose: Write a new email.
- Greeting: The opening line (Hello, Hi).
- Body: The main message.
- Closing: The ending line (Thank you).
- Signature: Your name and class.

### Core content
A good email has a clear structure: greeting, message, and closing. Start with "Hello" and the teacher's name. State your purpose in the first sentence. Keep the message short and polite. End with "Thank you" and your full name.
Check spelling and punctuation before you send. Avoid emojis in formal emails to teachers.

### Key reminders
- Use the teacher's name in the greeting.
- Include your name at the end.

### Try it
- Write a short email asking about homework.
- Swap with a partner and check for greeting and closing.

### Quick check
1. What should the first sentence explain?
2. Why is a clear closing important?
3. Should you use emojis in emails to teachers?

### Did you know
Many people decide to open an email based on the subject line alone.

### Common pitfalls
- Forgetting to include your name.
- Writing a very long message.

### Exam tip
A clear email has greeting, body, and closing.

## Lesson 1.6.4: Adding and Managing Attachments
### Keywords
- Attachment: A file added to an email.
- Paperclip: The attachment icon.
- File size: How big a file is.
- OneDrive link: A shared link instead of a file.

### Core content
Click the paperclip icon to attach a file. Choose the file from OneDrive so it stays safe and updated. Check file size before sending.
If a file is too large, share a OneDrive link instead. Always mention the attachment in the email body so the reader knows to look for it.

### Key reminders
- Attach the file before you write "see attached".
- Use a OneDrive link for large files.

### Try it
- Attach a small file and check its size.
- Practice inserting a OneDrive link.

### Quick check
1. What icon is used to add an attachment?
2. What should you do if a file is too large?
3. Why should you mention the attachment in the email?

### Did you know
The first email attachment was a photo sent in 1992.

### Common pitfalls
- Forgetting to attach a file after mentioning it.

### Exam tip
Use the paperclip icon to add an attachment.

## Lesson 1.6.5: Inbox Management and Digital Citizenship
### Keywords
- Inbox: Where new emails arrive.
- Delete: Remove emails you do not need.
- Archive: Store important emails safely.
- Digital citizenship: Safe and respectful behavior online.
- Signature: Your name at the end of an email.

### Core content
Keep your inbox tidy by deleting old messages and archiving important ones. Empty the Deleted Items folder so it does not fill up.
Be polite and respectful in all messages. Use school email for school work only. If you receive a strange or uncomfortable email, tell a teacher.

### Key reminders
- A clean inbox is easier to use.
- Report uncomfortable content to a teacher.

### Try it
- Sort a few emails into Delete or Archive.
- Write a short email signature with your name and class.

### Quick check
1. Why should you archive important emails?
2. What should you do with strange emails?
3. What is digital citizenship?

### Did you know
Many adults receive over 100 emails a day, so good habits matter.

### Common pitfalls
- Never deleting emails.
- Using school email for non-school messages.

### Exam tip
Digital citizenship means being safe and respectful online.
